On 24 September 2026 The Graph Foundation told developers in the `#indexers` channel that
query traffic from the Subgraph Studio staging environment is moving onto The Graph Network,
starting with **BNB Smart Chain and Polygon on 8 October 2026**. Studio support for those
subgraphs is not cut entirely until **31 October 2026**. About 450 subgraphs may be affected,
and the Foundation expects not all of them to migrate.

Two further points from the same announcement matter to you. The Foundation will **not
publish the list** of affected subgraphs; a bot detects published BNB and Polygon subgraphs
and curates on them, and the Foundation posts which ones need indexing as it goes. And that
curation is guaranteed only through 8 October, from a fixed budget that is meant to skip
clones and subgraphs nobody queries.

<Note title="Announced, not yet happened">
Everything above is a plan with dates attached, stated by the Foundation on 24 September.
This page was written before 8 October. If you are reading it afterwards, the tracker at
[graph-support #45](https://github.com/nightswatchhq/graph-support/issues/45) holds what
actually happened, including whether the dates held.
</Note>

## What you have today, and what you are moving to.

In Studio you deployed with a **deploy key**, and the docs describe what it is for: it
"allows you to publish your Subgraphs or manage your API keys and billing." Studio then
gave you a development query URL. That URL is, in the quick start's words, "rate-limited,
not visible to the public, and meant to be used for development, staging, and testing
purposes", and the limit is <Param name="studio_dev_query_limit" />. If your application
is reading it in production, this is the traffic that is moving.

On the network you query a gateway with an **API key**, and the docs give the shape:
`https://gateway.thegraph.com/api/<YOUR_API_KEY>/subgraphs/id/<SUBGRAPH_ID>`. The key is a
separate thing from the deploy key, created under API Keys in Studio, and it spends GRT from
a balance you top up. The [querying entry](/developers/query-from-an-app/) covers what
sits behind that URL and why the key must never ship in a client.

<Flow steps={["deploy to Studio", "dev URL", "publish", "signal", "allocation", "gateway URL"]} hot={4} />

The step this migration adds nothing to, and everything depends on, is the fifth. A
published subgraph is served by whichever indexers choose to allocate to it. Signal makes
that choice attractive. It does not make it. The
[building and publishing entry](/developers/build-a-subgraph/) explains why the protocol is
built that way; here the question is only what you do about it in the next two weeks.

## Publishing.

The docs give the two routes. From Studio: "Go to the Subgraph Studio dashboard. Click on
the **Publish** button. Your Subgraph will now be visible in Graph Explorer." From the CLI:
"As of version 0.73.0, you can also publish your Subgraph with the graph-cli", by running
`graph codegen && graph build` and then `graph publish`, which opens a wallet connection to
sign the transaction. Studio also lets you add signal "in the same transaction it is
published", and suggests <Param name="recommended_dev_signal" /> as a starting figure.

For BNB and Polygon subgraphs during this migration, the Foundation's bot is meant to add
signal for you. Do not rely on it. It is budgeted, it is guaranteed only until 8 October,
and it is meant to skip clones. If your subgraph is a republish of somebody else's
deployment, or has never been queried, expect nothing from it and signal yourself.

<Warn title="Keep the Studio endpoint alive until the network one has answered">
Nothing about publishing switches your application over. The Studio URL keeps working
until the Foundation stops it, and the network URL starts working only once an indexer
has allocated and synced. Point production at the gateway when you have seen it return
data, and not before. Between those two moments, keeping both costs you nothing.

The date to hold in mind is 31 October, when Studio stops regardless of whether the
network is serving you.
</Warn>

## The first two hours, read off the gateway.

Immediately after publishing, queries through the gateway will fail, and the failures
change shape as indexers pick the subgraph up. The exact strings are catalogued in the
[gateway error reference](https://github.com/nightswatchhq/graph-support/blob/main/reference/gateway-errors.md);
these are the three you will meet.

**`no indexers found`** means nobody is allocated to this deployment. There was nothing
to try. In the first minutes this is expected. An hour after signal landed it is the
failure this whole migration is about: curation arrived and no indexer acted on it. Check
allocations on the deployment, not signal. Signal in Graph Explorer looks like progress
and is not.

**`bad indexers: {0x…: Unavailable(too far behind)}`** is the good case. An indexer has
allocated and is syncing, and the gateway is refusing to route to it until it is near chain
head. On a subgraph with long history this can last hours. Wait.

**`bad indexers: {0x…: BadResponse(no attestation: indexing_error)}`** on every allocated
indexer means the subgraph itself fails deterministically at some block. Every indexer
that reaches that block records the same error, and graph-node then refuses to answer
queries against a deployment holding one. Studio's upgrade indexer may have been serving
you a copy that looked healthy while carrying exactly that error. Three recent cases with
the full diagnosis: [#32](https://github.com/nightswatchhq/graph-support/issues/32),
[#37](https://github.com/nightswatchhq/graph-support/issues/37) and
[#39](https://github.com/nightswatchhq/graph-support/issues/39).

The distinguishing check between the last two is the same each time: ask two allocated
indexers for the deployment's indexing status and compare. Both far behind is syncing. Both
failed at the same block with the same message is your mapping. One failed and one fine is
that operator's node, not you.

<Model>
Every one of these is the gateway telling you, in its own vocabulary, which of three
parties is not ready: no indexer has chosen you, an indexer has chosen you and is not
there yet, or every indexer has reached the same conclusion about your code. Learn to read
the map after `bad indexers:` and you do not need anybody to interpret it for you.
</Model>

## One line about line endings.

On 16 September 2026 a PancakeSwap v3 subgraph on BSC was published with every line of its
manifest ending in `\r\n`, and a parser in The Graph's own network subgraph fed the
`\r` to `BigInt.fromString`. That took the network subgraph, and the gateway, down for
three hours ([#41](https://github.com/nightswatchhq/graph-support/issues/41)). That parser
has been fixed. A few hundred manifests are nevertheless about to be published from a few
hundred machines, so before `graph publish`, run `grep -c $'\r$' subgraph.yaml` and expect
`0`.

<Reveal question="Before reading on: your subgraph was signalled by the Foundation's bot on 9 October, and on 10 October the gateway still says no indexers found. What are the two most likely reasons, and which one can you fix?">
The first is that no indexer has chosen to allocate. Signal makes a subgraph attractive
relative to every other signalled subgraph, and a fixed curation budget spread across
hundreds of migrating subgraphs may not have made yours attractive enough. You can fix this
by adding your own signal, and by asking in the indexer channels, where operators are
usually the fastest responders in the ecosystem.

The second is that an indexer did allocate, and closed again, because the subgraph failed
deterministically the moment it synced to the bad block. Then the error would have been
`bad indexers` with `no attestation: indexing_error` for a while, before returning to
`no indexers found` when the allocation closed. You can fix this too, but it is a mapping
fix and a new version, not a signal.

The thing you cannot fix is the one to rule out first: that the deployment hash you are
querying is not the one that was published. Deploying to Studio and publishing to the
network are different acts, and it is the published hash that carries the signal.
</Reveal>
