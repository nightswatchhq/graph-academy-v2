By late August 2026 the Council had voted not to designate Edge & Node as a "Labs" entity
for The Graph. It is the decision that produced [the new mandate](/governance/the-new-mandate/),
and through it the redirect of <Param name="innovation_allocation_pct" /> of issuance.

It is also the cleanest example this site has of
[a decision that took the meeting route](/governance/what-the-record-shows/). This entry
sets out what can be read about it, where, and what cannot be read anywhere.

## What was said, and by whom

The account below is the Foundation's, given aloud on Indexer Office Hours #272 and
recorded in
[community notes posted to the forum the next day](https://forum.thegraph.com/t/tldr-for-ioh-272-the-foundations-new-mandate/7036),
2026-08-26. The notes are Nuthatch's, the same people who maintain this site.

- The agreements with core developer teams, described as more than $150 million in
  total, were financially untenable, and attempts to realign them had failed.
- Edge & Node was the obvious partner for a Labs-style entity. It declined in late
  2025. The Foundation wrote its own roadmap in February 2026. Edge & Node returned
  with a counter-proposal, and after months of negotiation the Council voted no.
- Edge & Node is transitioning its network operations, and their costs, to the
  Foundation.

That is one party's telling of a negotiation with two parties in it. Hold it as that.

## What the vote settled

**There is no Labs entity.** The Foundation is becoming the operator itself, which is
the change the mandate post describes.

**The operations moved.** By the
[call of 2026-09-01](https://forum.thegraph.com/t/last-ioh-tldr-transition-week-update/7054)
the handover was described as more than 50 platforms and accounts, plus oracles,
bridges, contracts, wallets, API keys, monitoring, docs and playbooks. Chain
integration deals followed, per
[the notes for Graph Office Hours #274](https://forum.thegraph.com/t/tl-dr-graph-office-hours-274-first-under-the-new-name/7062).

**It is being paid for out of issuance.** GIP-0089 is the funding half of the same
decision, and [the mandate entry](/governance/the-new-mandate/) covers it.

## What it did not settle, because none of it was published

- **What was proposed.** Neither the Labs proposal nor the counter-proposal has been
  published. We found no GIP in
  [the repository](https://github.com/graphprotocol/graph-improvement-proposals) that
  proposes a Labs designation.
- **The vote.** No count, no date and no reasoning. The newest Council minute on the
  forum as of 2026-10-01 is
  [Meeting #122](https://forum.thegraph.com/t/graph-council-meeting-122/6991), held on
  2026-06-04, and its Council Business section is one sentence about the roadmap.
- **Edge & Node's account.** None in a form that can be cited, which the next section
  explains.
- **What Edge & Node does next**, and what becomes of the plan to bring its product Amp
  onto the network as a data service. Nobody has said in public.

## Where each account lives

This is the part worth the entry. Take the sources one at a time and ask of each: is it
a page, and does it name the decision?

**The mandate post** is a page, published 2026-08-19. It names neither Edge & Node nor
a Labs entity. Its FAQ says the change "was not a unilateral move" and that the
Foundation "was encouraged to rethink the mandate by the Council and by other core
teams in the ecosystem". The vote that prompted it is not in it.

**The Foundation's account** was spoken on a call. It survives as a recording and as
other people's notes.

**The other account** was posted in Discord by Ford, a long-standing contributor. The
forum thread establishes that it exists and that it differs: a community member
thanked him for it on 2026-08-30 and asked the Foundation why no agreement had been
reached.

**The Foundation's reply** is a page, posted the same day by Nick. He wrote: "I do not
agree with the perspective he shared in his Discord post." He declined to argue it in
writing, "I'd rather not get into a Forum/Discord posts debate", offered a community
event in Discord instead, and added: "can you tell me the specific questions you want
answers to and I will do my best to share an answer here."

**The specific questions** then went to Discord. The asker's last forum post says so,
and the thread ends there.

<Flow steps={["question: forum", "answer: Discord", "follow-up: Discord", "decision: a meeting"]} sep="·" />

So the fullest explanation available to the public sits in a chat client, on both
sides. The one durable artefact is a thread containing a question, a disagreement and
a pointer elsewhere.

<Warn title="What this entry does not carry">
It does not reproduce either Discord account, and it does not say which is right.

A chat message can be edited or deleted, has no stable address for most readers, and
was not written as a statement of record. Quoting it here would turn it into one
without its author's consent, and paraphrasing it would be worse. If either party
publishes a written account, this entry should link it, and that would be a good
[contribution](/contribute/).
</Warn>

## The same weakness, once more: who the team is

The mandate rests on an in-house team. Its size was first given in Discord. The names
came later, on calls, and are in community notes: Pedro as Head of Product and Orion as
Head of Protocol and Network, then Tomas, Miguel, Carlos, Samuel and Juan. We have not
found a Foundation page that lists them. The Foundation's
[Q3 review](https://thegraph.com/blog/q3-2026-in-review/) says engineers from GraphOps
and Edge & Node joined, and names nobody.

Nothing suggests the notes are wrong. The point is the one
[the mandate entry](/governance/the-new-mandate/) makes about the roadmap: a fact that
lives in a call can be neither cited nor diffed.

<Model>
For any governance outcome, list the accounts of it and mark each one: page, call or
chat. Then mark which of them names the decision. If the only pages are the ones that
do not name it, you know what happened and you do not know why, and those are
different amounts of knowledge.
</Model>

## What would close it

All three are cheap, and none requires anyone to reveal a vote.

- **A minute for the meeting**, with the outcome and a sentence of reasoning.
- **The proposals themselves**, published after the fact. A declined proposal is still
  worth reading, as [GIP-0058](/governance/the-withdrawn-proposal/) shows.
- **One written account from each party.** Two pages that disagree are more use than
  two chat posts that disagree, because the reader can put them side by side.

<Reveal question="Before reading on: if the Foundation's account is probably accurate, why does it matter that it was only spoken?">
Because "probably accurate" is doing the work that a document should be doing.

A spoken account reaches the people on the call. Everyone else gets a summary written
by whoever took notes, and here the note-taker is a party with interests of its own.
The summary may be faithful. A reader has no way to tell, short of finding the
recording and listening to it, and most will not.

It also cannot be answered. Edge & Node can agree or disagree with a page, line by
line, and a reader can watch them do it. Nobody can do that with a recording and a
chat post. The disagreement stays real and stays unexaminable.

None of this needs bad faith from anyone. A negotiation that ended without agreement
will have two honest accounts of why. The public is owed both, written down, and at
the moment has neither.
</Reveal>
