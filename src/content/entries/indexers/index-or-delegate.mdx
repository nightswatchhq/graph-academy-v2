Most guides to running an indexer assume you have already decided. This one does
not, because the decision is the expensive part and the software is not.

## The baseline you are actually beating.

You have at least <Param name="min_indexer_self_stake" />. You could delegate it to
an existing indexer, do nothing, and take a return.

So indexing is not a choice between earning and not earning. It is a choice between
a passive return and an active one, where the active one costs you infrastructure,
attention, and the risk of doing it badly. The question is whether the difference
covers all three.

<Warn title="Be honest about the comparison">
The right comparison is not "indexing rewards versus zero". It is "what I earn as an
indexer, minus infrastructure, minus my own time, minus the cost of mistakes,
against what I would earn delegating the same capital while doing nothing at all".

Run that comparison before you buy hardware, not after. Note also that
<Param name="innovation_allocation_pct" /> of protocol issuance was redirected as of
2026-08-31, so indexing reward figures gathered before that date overstate the
current pot for both sides of the comparison.
</Warn>

## Where an indexer's advantage actually comes from.

Three places, and it is worth being clear that only one of them scales with effort.

**Delegation.** You can accept up to <Param name="max_delegation_ratio" /> your
self-stake in delegated GRT, and you keep a cut of what that capital earns. This is
the largest structural advantage in the role. An indexer earning a cut on many times
their own capital is running a different business from one earning on their own
stake alone.

It is also the hardest part to obtain. Delegators choose operators with a track
record, and you do not have one yet.

**Query fees.** Real revenue, dependent on serving queries the gateway routes to
you, which depends on your pricing and performance.

**Better allocation than the average.** If you understand which subgraphs are worth
indexing better than the operator you would otherwise delegate to, you capture that
difference. This is genuine and it is also the claim most people make without
evidence.

## The case for indexing.

Sound reasons, in roughly descending order:

- **You already run infrastructure.** If you operate chain nodes for another
  purpose, much of the marginal cost is already paid.
- **You want the delegation business.** The economics work when you are earning a
  cut on other people's capital, and that requires being an indexer.
- **You have a data thesis.** You believe you can pick subgraphs better than the
  field, and you can say why.
- **You want to serve a specific application well.** Being the indexer your own
  product depends on is a legitimate reason that has nothing to do with yield.
- **You want to strengthen the network.** Also legitimate, and worth being explicit
  that it is a contribution rather than a trade.

## The case against.

Equally sound, and more often correct:

- **You want passive income.** Indexing is not passive. Delegation is.
- **You are at the minimum stake with no delegation.** Fixed costs fall on a small
  base, and the arithmetic is hostile.
- **You cannot respond to problems within hours.** A broken deployment earns nothing
  and looks fine from outside. Stake behind an unnoticed failure is stake earning
  zero.
- **You have not priced archive access.** This is the cost that surprises people, and
  it recurs monthly.
- **You would be doing it alone with no fallback.** Holidays exist. Illness exists.
  The chain does not pause for either.

<Model>
Indexing is a small infrastructure business whose main product is credibility with
delegators. If you would not want to run a small infrastructure business, the
protocol has a role that pays you for capital alone, and choosing it is not a
lesser outcome.
</Model>

## If the answer is yes, start narrow.

- One chain. The one you know best.
- A handful of subgraphs you have reasons for, not a list scraped by signal rank.
- Monitoring before scale. Knowing a deployment has failed matters more than
  supporting another network.
- Publish under a name, in the forum, from the start. The delegation business runs on
  being a known quantity, and that takes longer to build than the infrastructure
  does.

## If the answer is no, that is a result.

Delegating is a real role with real returns and no operational burden. Deciding
against indexing after working through the numbers is a better outcome than
discovering the same thing eight months in with idle hardware.

The [delegation collection](/delegators/) covers choosing an operator properly, and you
will now be unusually good at it, because you know what the job involves.
