Two parts. Work the decision honestly, then plan the monitoring you would need before
committing anything.

### Before you commit stake

- Write down your capital and compare it against the protocol minimum. State plainly whether you can also accept delegation, and how you intend to attract any.
- Price archive access for every chain you intend to index, monthly, as a real number.
- Compute what the same capital would earn delegated to an existing indexer, doing nothing. That is the number you must beat.
- Pick three subgraphs you would allocate to and write one sentence each on why, without referring to their signal rank.
- Open a dashboard and check whether those three are already crowded with allocated stake.
- List the four silent failure modes from this collection and say, for each, what alert you would build.
- Decide your max_amount_willing_to_lose_grt and write down the reasoning, not just the number.
- Name the person who covers your alerts when you are unavailable. If there is no name, that is your finding.


## An honest closing note

<Warn>
This collection deliberately spends more effort on economics and failure modes than on
installation, because installation is documented elsewhere and is not where operators
lose money.

If working through the checklist above produced more discomfort than enthusiasm, that
is a useful result rather than a failure. The [delegation collection](/delegators/) covers a
role that pays for capital without the operational burden, and you will now evaluate
indexers far better than most people who delegate.
</Warn>
